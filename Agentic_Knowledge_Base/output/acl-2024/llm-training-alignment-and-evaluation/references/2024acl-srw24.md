# Exploring the Effectiveness and Consistency of Task Selection in Intermediate-Task Transfer Learning

**Source**: [https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-srw.24/](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-srw.24/)

**TLDR**: Investigates task selection strategies for intermediate-task transfer learning across 130 source-target combinations, revealing high variance in transfer performance.

## Abstract

AbstractIdentifying beneficial tasks to transfer from is a critical step toward successful intermediate-task transfer learning. In this work, we experiment with 130 source-target task combinations and demonstrate that the transfer performance exhibits severe variance across different source tasks and training seeds, highlighting the crucial role of intermediate-task selection in a broader context. We compare four representative task selection methods in a unified setup, focusing on their effectiveness and consistency. Compared to embedding-free methods and text embeddings, task embeddings constructed from fine-tuned weights can better estimate task transferability by improving task prediction scores from 2.59% to 3.96%. Despite their strong performance, we observe that the task embeddings do not consistently demonstrate superiority for tasks requiring reasoning abilities. Furthermore, we introduce a novel method that measures pairwise token similarity using maximum inner product search, leading to the highest performance in task prediction. Our findings suggest that token-wise similarity is better predictive for predicting transferability compared to averaging weights.
