# X-Oscar: A Progressive Framework for High-quality Text-guided 3D Animatable Avatar Generation

**Source**: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/ma24g.html

## [POSITIVE] Progressive Modeling (Geometry→Texture→Animation)
A sequential three-stage pipeline that breaks avatar generation into geometry modeling, appearance modeling, and animation refinement, optimizing only a subset of parameters at each stage.

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Text-guided 3D animatable avatar generation; ablation comparing joint vs. sequential optimization

**Evidence**: "The results shown in Fig. 7 reveal a significant enhancement in the quality of geometry and appearance in the generated avatars when using the progressive modeling paradigm. For example, consider the prompt 'Albert Einstein'. Without employing the progressive modeling approach, the generated avatar is limited to a rudimentary shape and color, lacking the intricate details necessary for recognizing Albert Einstein."

## [POSITIVE] Adaptive Variational Parameter (AVP)
Represents avatar geometry and appearance as adaptive distributions (using standard deviations of vertex offsets and albedo maps as perturbation weights) rather than specific parameters, to mitigate oversaturation.

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Geometry and appearance modeling stages; addresses oversaturation in SDS-based optimization

**Evidence**: "our observations strongly indicate that the omission of AVP in X-Oscar can lead to an excessive optimization of geometry and appearance, as an effort to align the generated outputs with the text. This subsequently leads to the problem of oversaturation. Geometry oversaturation leads to topological overlay problems in the generated meshes, while appearance oversaturation results in avatars with exaggerated color contrast. By integrating AVP, we successfully tackle these issues, significantly improving the realism of both the geometry and appearance in the generated avatars."

## [POSITIVE] Avatar-aware Score Distillation Sampling (ASDS)
Incorporates geometry-aware and appearance-aware noise (derived from the standard deviations of vertex offsets and albedo maps) into the rendered image during SDS optimization, creating a curriculum from easy to difficult denoising.

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: All three training stages; replaces standard random Gaussian noise in SDS

**Evidence**: "the absence of ASDS directly results in a noticeable decline in the overall quality of both the geometry and appearance of the generated avatars. For instance, without ASDS, two ears on Batman's head exhibit a geometric merging phenomenon. In the case of Mulan, the facial details become blurred and the colors on the front and back of the pants are inconsistent."

## [POSITIVE] SMPL-X Body Model as Prior
Uses the SMPL-X parametric human body model (10,475 vertices, 54 joints) as the 3D representation backbone, providing human shape, pose, and expression priors.

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Compared to text-to-3D methods without human priors (DreamFusion, Magic3D, Fantasia3D, ProlificDreamer)

**Evidence**: "owing to the absence of human prior knowledge, the avatars generated by text-to-3D methods lack flexibility and pose challenges in terms of animation. In contrast, our proposed method excels in generating high-quality, animatable avatars."

## [POSITIVE] Mesh-based 3D Representation
Uses explicit mesh representation (SMPL-X mesh with vertex offsets and albedo map) instead of NeRF or Gaussian Splatting.

**Delta**: 172 minutes vs 69 minutes (HumanGaussian), 105 minutes (DreamWaltz), 156 minutes (AvatarCLIP)
**Condition**: Among mesh-based methods using Stable Diffusion; X-Oscar is fastest among mesh+SD methods at 172 min vs TADA 175 min and AvatarCraft 298 min

**Evidence**: "In the case of Mesh representation combined with Stable Diffusion as the guiding model, our proposed method demonstrates the shortest training time. This can be attributed to our proposed progressive framework, which optimizes only a portion of the parameters during the geometric and appearance modeling stages."

## [POSITIVE] Animation Refinement Stage with Pose Sampling
A third training stage that samples diverse poses from VPoser and simultaneously optimizes geometry and appearance in animated poses to improve quality in occluded regions.

**Delta**: outperforms baseline
**Condition**: Final stage of progressive pipeline; addresses occlusion artifacts from canonical-pose-only optimization

**Evidence**: "Given that both the geometry modeling and appearance modeling stages optimize the avatar in a canonical pose, it is inevitable that certain parts of the avatar may be obstructed, leading to lower-quality results in those areas. To overcome this challenge, we introduce an animation refinement stage where we adjust the pose of the avatar and simultaneously optimize both the geometry and appearance."

## [POSITIVE] Facial Detail Enhancement Strategy
During training, applies a 0.2 probability of rendering facial close-up images and 0.8 probability of full-body images for optimization.

**Delta**: descriptive improvement in facial detail
**Condition**: All training stages; implementation detail for improving facial quality

**Evidence**: "To enhance facial details, we employ a strategy where there is a 0.2 probability of rendering facial images for optimization during the training process, and a 0.8 probability of rendering full-body images for optimization."

## [NEGATIVE] Standard SDS with Random Gaussian Noise (baseline)
Original Score Distillation Sampling that adds random Gaussian noise independent of the avatar's current state, used as the baseline comparison for ASDS.

**Delta**: lower quality geometry and appearance vs ASDS
**Condition**: When used without avatar-aware noise modification; leads to geometric merging and blurred facial details

**Evidence**: "Since t ∼ U(0.02, 0.98) is randomly sampled, the noise added to the rendered image is independent of the avatar's current state... the absence of ASDS directly results in a noticeable decline in the overall quality of both the geometry and appearance of the generated avatars."

## [NEGATIVE] Fixed Perturbation Weights (non-adaptive)
Using manually set fixed scalar weights λv and λa for perturbation instead of adaptive standard-deviation-based weights, as a design alternative to AVP.

**Delta**: suboptimal; requires manual tuning
**Condition**: Alternative to AVP; problematic at both extremes (too small → no benefit, too large → unstable)

**Evidence**: "choosing appropriate values for λv and λa is crucial and challenging. If these values are too small, the model may not fully benefit from learning the distributions... Conversely, when λv and λa are excessively large, the learning process becomes challenging due to highly unstable perturbations."

## [POSITIVE] High-resolution Rendering and Albedo Map
Uses 800×800 pixel rendered images and 2048×2048 pixel albedo maps during training.

**Delta**: descriptive quality improvement
**Condition**: All training stages; contributes to high-quality texture output

**Evidence**: "During the training phase, we set the resolution of the rendered images to 800 × 800 pixels. The resolution of the albedo map is 2048 × 2048 pixels."

## [POSITIVE] X-Oscar vs. Text-to-Avatar Baselines (User Study)
Overall framework comparison against SOTA text-to-avatar methods on geometry quality, texture quality, and text consistency.

**Delta**: Geo. Qua.: 8.85, Tex. Qua.: 8.91, Tex. Con.: 9.22 vs best baseline TADA at 6.95, 7.62 (partial scores visible)
**Condition**: User study with 52 participants, 40 ChatGPT-generated prompts; compared against DreamFusion, Magic3D, Fantasia3D, ProlificDreamer, AvatarCLIP, AvatarCraft, DreamWaltz, HumanGaussian, TADA

**Evidence**: "our method consistently outperforms all other methods across all evaluated aspects... X-Oscar consistently achieves either the best or second-best results, demonstrating its ability to generate 3D avatars that are semantically consistent with the provided text prompts."
