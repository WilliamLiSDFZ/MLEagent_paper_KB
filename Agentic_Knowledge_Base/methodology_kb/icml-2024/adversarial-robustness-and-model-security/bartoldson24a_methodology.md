# Adversarial Robustness Limits via Scaling-Law and Human-Alignment Studies

**Source**: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/bartoldson24a.html

## [POSITIVE] Scaling Laws for Adversarial Robustness
Developing empirical scaling laws that model how model size (N), dataset size (D), and synthetic data quality (FID) affect adversarial robustness, enabling compute-optimal training configurations.

**Delta**: +3% AutoAttack accuracy over prior SOTA, 20% fewer training FLOPs
**Condition**: CIFAR10 adversarial robustness with ℓ∞-norm bounded perturbations

**Evidence**: "By applying actionable guidelines from our scaling laws, compared to the prior SOTA (Wang et al., 2023), we achieve the same robustness level with 20% fewer FLOPs, enable a +1% AutoAttack gain with a 3× smaller model trained with the same compute budget, and set a new SOTA (+3% AutoAttack accuracy) using a larger compute budget."

## [POSITIVE] Compute-Optimal Model and Dataset Size Selection
Using scaling law predictions to select the optimal model size N* and dataset size D* for a given compute budget, rather than using oversized models as in prior SOTA.

**Delta**: +1% AutoAttack accuracy with 3× smaller model at same compute budget
**Condition**: When prior SOTA used a model too large for its training FLOPs budget

**Evidence**: "A model predicted to be compute-optimal at the prior SOTA's training FLOPs budget (WideResNet-82-8, 79M parameters) beats the prior SOTA (WideResNet70-16, 267M parameters) by 1% AutoAttack while being over 3× more FLOP and parameter efficient."

## [POSITIVE] Large-Scale Synthetic Data Training (300M samples)
Training on up to 300M unique synthetic CIFAR10 data points generated by diffusion models, seeing each example only once (single epoch).

**Delta**: New SOTA of 73.71% AutoAttack accuracy
**Condition**: Larger compute budget with WideResNet-94-16

**Evidence**: "A new SOTA of 73.71% AutoAttack accuracy with a WideResNet-94-16 trained on 500M samples."

## [POSITIVE] Synthetic-Data-Only Adversarial Training
Training entirely on synthetic data without any real CIFAR10 images, using a single epoch over the synthetic dataset.

**Delta**: 71.7% AutoAttack accuracy, surpassing prior SOTA
**Condition**: Single-epoch training on synthetic data only, no real CIFAR10 data

**Evidence**: "A model trained entirely on synthetic data outperforms the prior SOTA, reaching 71.7% AutoAttack accuracy."

## [POSITIVE] High-Quality Synthetic Data (Low FID)
Using synthetic data generators with lower FID scores (higher quality), such as EDM with more diffusion steps, PFGM++, and Discriminator Guidance (DG), to improve adversarial training.

**Delta**: FID improvement from 35.54 (EDM-5) to 1.65 (DG) correlates with improved robustness
**Condition**: Scaling adversarial training with synthetic datasets

**Evidence**: "Given the benefit of training on higher quality data observed in all of our approaches (e.g., Figure 1), we boost dataset quality for these training runs by mixing CIFAR10 data in with the synthetic data."

## [POSITIVE] Real Data Mixing with Synthetic Data
Mixing real CIFAR10 examples into synthetic training data at a ratio of 1 real example per 7 synthetic examples to boost effective data quality.

**Delta**: Contributes to SOTA results; models with mixing outperform synthetic-only baseline
**Condition**: Training runs targeting highest robustness levels

**Evidence**: "We boost dataset quality for these training runs by mixing CIFAR10 data in with the synthetic data at a rate of 1 CIFAR10 example for every 7 synthetic examples. Such mixing was also done to obtain the prior SOTA (Wang et al., 2023)."

## [POSITIVE] 10-Step PGD Adversarial Training
Using 10 projected gradient descent steps to adversarially attack images at training time, making each adversarial training iteration approximately 9× more expensive than regular training.

**Delta**: Standard practice enabling SOTA robustness levels around 70%+
**Condition**: Adversarial training on CIFAR10 with ℓ∞ perturbations

**Evidence**: "It is common practice to adversarially train on CIFAR10 data by modifying training data via a 10-step attack (Rebuffi et al., 2021; Sehwag et al., 2021; Wang et al., 2023; Peng et al., 2023), making an adversarial training iteration 9× as expensive as a regular one."

## [POSITIVE] TRADES Loss
Using the TRADES loss function with β=5 to balance clean accuracy and adversarial robustness during training.

**Delta**: Part of SOTA training setup achieving 73.71% AutoAttack accuracy
**Condition**: Adversarial training on CIFAR10

**Evidence**: "the TRADES loss (Zhang et al., 2019) with β = 5"

## [POSITIVE] Weight Averaging (SWA)
Applying stochastic weight averaging with decay rate τ=0.995 during adversarial training.

**Delta**: Part of SOTA training setup
**Condition**: Adversarial training on CIFAR10

**Evidence**: "weight averaging (Izmailov et al., 2018) with decay rate τ = 0.995"

## [POSITIVE] Cyclic Learning Rate with Cosine Annealing
Using a cyclic learning rate schedule with cosine annealing during adversarial training.

**Delta**: Part of SOTA training setup
**Condition**: Adversarial training on CIFAR10

**Evidence**: "a cyclic learning rate schedule with cosine annealing (Smith & Topin, 2017)"

## [POSITIVE] Label Smoothing
Applying label smoothing of 0.1 during adversarial training.

**Delta**: Part of SOTA training setup
**Condition**: Adversarial training on CIFAR10

**Evidence**: "label smoothing (Szegedy et al., 2016) 0.1"

## [POSITIVE] SiLU Activations in WideResNet
Using SiLU (Sigmoid Linear Unit) activations in all WideResNet models instead of standard ReLU.

**Delta**: Part of SOTA training setup
**Condition**: WideResNet models for adversarial training

**Evidence**: "All models use SiLU activations (Hendrycks & Gimpel, 2016)."

## [NEGATIVE] Oversized Model Relative to Compute Budget (Prior SOTA)
Using a model that is too large for the given training FLOPs budget, as done by Wang et al. (2023) with WideResNet-70-16 (267M parameters).

**Delta**: Uses 20% more training FLOPs than necessary for same robustness level
**Condition**: Prior SOTA configuration (Wang et al., 2023)

**Evidence**: "all of our scaling laws agree that the prior SOTA was trained with too large a model, which is just outside the compute-efficient region in Figure 5... we matched this prior robustness peak with 20% fewer training FLOPs."

## [NEGATIVE] Scaling Compute Alone (Without Efficiency Improvements)
Simply increasing compute budget without improving training efficiency or architecture, relying purely on scale to improve robustness.

**Delta**: Would require ~10^30 FLOPs (~3,000 years on 25,000 GPUs) to reach human performance
**Condition**: Current adversarial training approaches on CIFAR10

**Evidence**: "even with unlimited CIFAR10 data, we estimate that reaching human performance would require roughly 10^30 FLOPs, about 3,000 years of TF32 matrix math on 25,000 MI300 or H100 GPUs. Therefore, meaningful progress on this problem requires the design of more efficient training algorithms and improved architectures, rather than simply scaling."

## [NEGATIVE] ℓ∞-Norm Bounded Attack Formulation
Using ℓ∞-norm constraints (ε=8/255) to bound adversarial perturbations during benchmarking, which can generate invalid images that no longer fit their original label.

**Delta**: ~10% of adversarial benchmark images are invalid; human performance plateaus at ~90%
**Condition**: AutoAttack benchmarking on CIFAR10 with ℓ∞ perturbations

**Evidence**: "we estimate that human performance also plateaus near 90%, which we show to be attributable to ℓ∞-constrained attacks' generation of invalid images not consistent with their original labels... Human performance suggests that 727/2629 images are 'invalid'."

## [NEUTRAL] Approach 1 Scaling Law (Envelope of Training Curves)
Fitting a power law to the envelope of training curves across model sizes and dataset sizes for a single data quality level, without modeling FID effects.

**Delta**: Cannot model effect of data quality or asymptotic performance limits
**Condition**: Single synthetic dataset quality; cannot extrapolate across FID values

**Evidence**: "Our first approach to understanding the scaling behavior of adversarial training... does not model the effect of training dataset FID on performance, so it is only fit to results associated with one training dataset."

## [POSITIVE] Approach 2 Scaling Law (Parametric Loss with Data Quality Terms)
Extending the Chinchilla parametric loss form to include FID-dependent terms (ε, ζ) that model how data quality affects irreducible loss and dataset scaling benefit.

**Delta**: Accurately predicts held-out datasets (EDM-6, PFGM++); predicts FID=0 asymptote ~90%
**Condition**: Datasets with moderate to high quality (FID ≤ ~14); poor fit for very low quality data (FID=35.54)

**Evidence**: "Approach 2 can predict performance on an FID=0 training dataset and the asymptotic performance as a function of FID... In Figure 3, we show how the fitted parameters accurately predict loss for the dataset used to fit the base parameters (DG), the datasets used to fit the quality parameters (EDM-7, EDM-10, EDM20), and two held out datasets (EDM-6, PFGM++)."

## [POSITIVE] Approach 3 Scaling Law (Data Quality Bottleneck Modeling)
A more complex parametric form that models a 'data quality bottleneck' coupling D with data quality, allowing simultaneous fitting across all dataset qualities including very low FID.

**Delta**: Accurately fits all datasets including EDM-5 (FID=35.54) where Approach 2 fails
**Condition**: All dataset quality levels including very low quality synthetic data

**Evidence**: "We fit Equation 5 using all datasets and models, finding L is accurately predicted due to the ability to model overfitting as a function of data quality... Approach 2 models quality rather simply, and empirically we find that the fit of this simple power law form is poor when the fitting includes the lowest quality dataset (EDM-5, FID=35.54)."

## [POSITIVE] Balanced N and D Scaling with High-Quality Data
Scaling model size and dataset size at roughly equal rates (a≈b≈0.5) when synthetic data quality is high, consistent with Chinchilla scaling laws.

**Delta**: a=0.56, b=0.43 for low quality (EDM-5); a=0.48, b=0.52 for high quality (PFGM++)
**Condition**: High-quality synthetic data (low FID); deviates for low-quality data

**Evidence**: "when data quality is sufficiently high, we find that efficient adversarial training scales the model size and dataset size at roughly the same rate, consistent with Chinchilla's scaling laws (Hoffmann et al., 2022)."

## [POSITIVE] Adaptive Batch Size and Learning Rate by Dataset Size
Using larger batch sizes (2048 vs 1024) and lower learning rates (0.1-0.2 vs 0.3) for larger datasets based on hyperparameter search.

**Delta**: Optimal settings found via hyperparameter study prior to scaling law experiments
**Condition**: Adversarial training with varying dataset sizes from 5M to 300M

**Evidence**: "The learning rate and batch size we use varies based on dataset size according to optimal settings found by hyperparameter search: datasets with 10M or fewer samples use batch size 1024, otherwise batch size is 2048; the learning rate is 0.3 for datasets with 10M or fewer samples, 0.2 for larger datasets with up to 200M samples, and 0.1 for datasets with 300M samples."

## [POSITIVE] Invalid Adversarial Data Removal from Benchmark
Removing images identified as invalid (no longer fitting original label after attack) from the benchmark test set to get a more accurate measure of robustness relative to human performance.

**Delta**: SOTA NN accuracy improves from 73.71% to 79.49% on valid data; estimated human+NN accuracy rises from 90.46% to 96.46%
**Condition**: Revised benchmarking excluding invalid adversarial images on CIFAR10

**Evidence**: "Table 4 shows our SOTA NN's robustness on the new and original benchmarks... SOTA NN: All Data 73.71%, Valid Data 79.49%; SOTA NN+Avg Human: All Data 90.46%, Valid Data 96.46%."

## [POSITIVE] Single-Epoch Training on Unique Synthetic Data
Generating enough synthetic data to see each example only once during training, mirroring LLM scaling law methodology and avoiding duplicate data effects.

**Delta**: Enables accurate scaling law fitting; synthetic-only model surpasses prior SOTA at 71.7%
**Condition**: Scaling law fitting and synthetic-only training runs

**Evidence**: "we generate enough data to only see each example once during training, allowing our scaling laws to reflect the benefit of adding a unique data point. This mirrors how LLM scaling laws are learned... we are the first to perform a single-epoch (i.e., seeing each image only once), synthetic-data-only adversarial training run that outperforms the prior state-of-the-art."
