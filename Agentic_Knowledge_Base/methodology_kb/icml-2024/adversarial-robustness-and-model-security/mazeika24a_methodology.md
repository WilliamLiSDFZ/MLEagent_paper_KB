# HarmBench: A Standardized Evaluation Framework for Automated Red Teaming and Robust Refusal

**Source**: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/mazeika24a.html

## [POSITIVE] Standardized Token Generation Length
Standardizing the number of tokens generated by the target model during evaluation to N=512 to ensure comparability across methods

**Delta**: up to 30% change in ASR if not standardized
**Condition**: Evaluation of red teaming methods using substring matching metrics

**Evidence**: "we found that the number of tokens generated during evaluation can have a drastic effect on ASR computed by substring matching metrics used in earlier work. We show in Figure 2 that the choice of this parameter can change ASR by up to 30%."

## [POSITIVE] Fine-tuned Llama 2 13B Classifier
Fine-tuning Llama 2 13B chat as an open-source classifier for evaluating whether test cases were successful

**Delta**: outperforms all existing classifiers
**Condition**: Evaluation of non-copyright behaviors in HarmBench

**Evidence**: "Our classifier obtains stronger performance than all existing classifiers. Moreover, ours is the only open-source classifier obtaining acceptable performance."

## [NEUTRAL] Hashing-based Copyright Classifier
A novel hashing-based classifier that directly checks whether copyrighted content was generated by comparing cryptographic hashes

**Delta**: lower ASR on copyright behaviors due to stricter standard
**Condition**: Evaluation of copyright behaviors specifically

**Evidence**: "On copyright behaviors, ASR is relatively low. This is because our hashing-based copyright classifier uses a stricter standard than our non-copyright classifiers, requiring that completions actually contain the copyrighted text to be labeled positive."

## [POSITIVE] Validation/Test Split for Behaviors
Providing an official validation/test split of HarmBench behaviors (100 validation, 410 test) to prevent gaming of evaluation metrics

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: Preventing overfitting/gaming of red teaming evaluations

**Evidence**: "a crucial measure to ensuring the robustness of evaluation metrics is using held-out classifiers and a validation/test split for harmful behaviors. We find that several prior works directly evaluate on the metric optimized by their method—a practice that can lead to substantial gaming."

## [POSITIVE] Contextual Behaviors Category
Behaviors consisting of a context string and a behavior string referencing the context, making them more realistic and differentially harmful

**Delta**: ASR is considerably higher on contextual behaviors; 0% searchability vs 50-55% for prior datasets
**Condition**: Evaluating differential harm and realistic attack scenarios

**Evidence**: "We find that ASR is considerably higher on contextual behaviors despite their increased potential for differential harm... The searchability rate was 55% for MaliciousInstruct, 50% for AdvBench, and 0% for HarmBench contextual behaviors, validating our design choice."

## [POSITIVE] Multimodal Behaviors Category
Behaviors consisting of an image and a behavior string referencing the image, enabling evaluation of multimodal LLMs

**Delta**: high ASR for PGD-based attacks, low ASR for Render Text baseline
**Condition**: Evaluation of multimodal LLMs

**Evidence**: "For multimodal results shown in Table 10 and Table 11, ASR is relatively high for PGD-based attacks but low for the Render Text baseline, corroborating findings in prior work."

## [POSITIVE] R2D2 Adversarial Training (Persistent Test Cases)
Robust Refusal Dynamic Defense: fine-tuning LLMs on a dynamic pool of test cases continually updated by GCG, using persistent test cases inspired by fast adversarial training literature

**Delta**: GCG ASR reduced from 31.8% (Llama 2 7B) and 30.2% (Llama 2 13B) to 5.9%; 4x lower ASR than second most robust model
**Condition**: Model-level defense against GCG and related attacks on open-source LLMs

**Evidence**: "Zephyr 7B + R2D2 obtains state-of-the-art robustness against GCG among model-level defenses, outperforming Llama 2 7B Chat (31.8 → 5.9) and Llama 2 13B Chat (30.2 → 5.9) in percent ASR... Compared to Zephyr 7B without R2D2, adding R2D2 uniformly improves robustness across all attacks."

## [NEGATIVE] R2D2 Adversarial Training - Generalization to Dissimilar Attacks
Training against GCG adversary and evaluating generalization to semantically different attack methods like PAIR, TAP, and Stochastic Few-Shot

**Delta**: less pronounced improvement for PAIR, TAP, and Stochastic Few-Shot
**Condition**: Generalization of adversarial training to attacks dissimilar to the training adversary

**Evidence**: "For some attacks, the improvement conferred by R2D2 is less pronounced. This is especially true for methods dissimilar to the train-time GCG adversary, including PAIR, TAP, and Stochastic Few-Shot."

## [POSITIVE] R2D2 Adversarial Training - General Capability Preservation
Using SFT loss with UltraChat dataset during adversarial training to preserve general conversational ability

**Delta**: MT-Bench score of 6.0 vs 6.5 for Mistral 7B Instruct v0.2 (minor degradation)
**Condition**: Maintaining general LLM performance while improving robustness

**Evidence**: "The MT-Bench scores of these models are 6.0 and 6.5, respectively. This suggests that adversarial training with automated red teaming can greatly improve robustness while preserving general performance."

## [NEGATIVE] Static Adversarial Training Dataset
Adversarial training using a fixed dataset of harmful prompts without updating the adversary during fine-tuning

**Delta**: reported negative results
**Condition**: Adversarial training for LLM robustness without dynamic test case generation

**Evidence**: "Prior work reported negative results using simpler forms of adversarial training (Jain et al., 2023)... Jain et al. (2023) conduct an adversarial training experiment with a static dataset of harmful prompts, in which the adversary does not optimize against the model during fine-tuning."

## [NEUTRAL] Model Size as Robustness Factor
Using larger model size to improve robustness to jailbreaks

**Delta**: no correlation found within model families
**Condition**: Within model families, across sizes from 7B to 70B parameters; exception noted for copyright behaviors

**Evidence**: "we find no correlation between robustness and model size within model families in our results. This is illustrated in Figure 6 across six model families, four red teaming methods, and model sizes ranging from 7 to 70 billion parameters."

## [POSITIVE] Greedy Decoding for Evaluation
Using deterministic greedy decoding for target model completions during evaluation to improve efficiency

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: Evaluation efficiency in red teaming benchmarks

**Evidence**: "To improve evaluation efficiency, we follow prior work in assuming that target models generate completions deterministically using greedy decoding."

## [POSITIVE] Differential Harm Filtering for Behaviors
Filtering behaviors based on whether they provide differential harm beyond what a search engine could accomplish

**Delta**: 0% searchability for HarmBench contextual behaviors vs 50-55% for prior datasets
**Condition**: Curation of harmful behaviors for red teaming benchmarks

**Evidence**: "The searchability rate was 55% for MaliciousInstruct, 50% for AdvBench, and 0% for HarmBench contextual behaviors, validating our design choice."

## [POSITIVE] Dual-Intent Behavior Filtering
Removing or altering candidate behaviors that could plausibly be performed for benign reasons to avoid ambiguous evaluations

**Delta**: None
**Condition**: Curation of unambiguous harmful behaviors for red teaming evaluation

**Evidence**: "In curating behaviors for HarmBench, we performed several filtering passes to remove or alter candidate behaviors such that it would be highly unlikely for most people to want an LLM to perform the behavior for benign reasons."

## [POSITIVE] Classifier Prequalification Tests
Testing classifiers on nonstandard completions including initial refusals followed by behavior, random benign paragraphs, and unrelated harmful behaviors

**Delta**: many previously used classifiers found to lack robustness
**Condition**: Selection of robust evaluation classifiers for red teaming

**Evidence**: "We compare a variety of classifiers on these sets in Table 5, finding that many previously used classifiers lack robustness to these simple but nonstandard scenarios."
