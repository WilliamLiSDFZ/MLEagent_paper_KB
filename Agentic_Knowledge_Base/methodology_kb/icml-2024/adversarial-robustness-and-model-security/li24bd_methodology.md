# Graph Neural Network Explanations are Fragile

**Source**: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v235/li24bd.html

## [POSITIVE] Loss-based attack
Uses the loss change induced by modifying an edge to indicate edge importance. Edges that largely increase the explanation loss when deleted or added are deemed important and selected for perturbation.

**Delta**: up to 64.80% overlap ratio on MUTAG with GNNExplainer
**Condition**: Attacking perturbation-based GNN explainers (GNNExplainer, PGExplainer, GSAT) across node and graph classification tasks

**Evidence**: "3) Deduction-based attack performs the best and loss-based attack performs the second best. This shows loss change is a good indicator to identify certain important edges for perturbation"

## [POSITIVE] Deduction-based attack
Simulates the dynamic mask learning process of perturbation-based GNN explainers to directly connect the attack to the objective. Rewrites the attack objective as a loss closely related to the explainer's loss and identifies important edges via this simulation.

**Delta**: 64.42% overlap ratio on Cycle with GNNExplainer, outperforming loss-based attack in most settings
**Condition**: Attacking perturbation-based GNN explainers; most effective when explanation loss is known

**Evidence**: "Deduction-based attack performs the best and loss-based attack performs the second best. This shows loss change is a good indicator to identify certain important edges for perturbation, while simulating the learning procedure of (black-box) GNN explainers can be more beneficial to identify the important edges"

## [NEGATIVE] Random attack baseline
Iteratively and randomly selects a pair of nodes to add or delete edges, subject to attack constraints such as likelihood ratio test and maintaining accurate prediction.

**Delta**: lowest overlap ratios across all datasets and explainers, e.g., 5.04% on House with GNNExplainer
**Condition**: Used as a baseline comparison for all GNN explainers and datasets

**Evidence**: "1) Random attack performs the worst, indicating randomly selecting edges for perturbation is not effective enough."

## [NEGATIVE] Kill-hot attack baseline
Assumes the adversary knows the mask generated by the explainer, selects non-explanatory edges with the highest mask values, and deletes the top-ξ edges.

**Delta**: slightly better than random but much worse than loss-based and deduction-based attacks, e.g., 11.11% vs 20.02% on House with GNNExplainer
**Condition**: Used as a baseline; requires knowledge of the explainer mask values

**Evidence**: "Kill-hot attack performs (slightly) better than random attack in most cases, but much worse than our two attacks. This implies the 'most important' non-explanatory edges contain some information to influence the final mask, but is not sufficient"

## [POSITIVE] Power-law likelihood ratio test constraint
Enforces structural similarity between the original and perturbed graph using a two-sample test for power-law distributions on degree distributions, requiring >99% structural similarity (Λ < 0.000157).

**Delta**: all perturbed graphs have similarity >0.95 with clean graphs under NetSim and DeltaCon metrics
**Condition**: Applied as an attack constraint to ensure stealthiness of generated perturbations

**Evidence**: "We found all the perturbed graphs generated by our deduction-based attack and the respective clean graphs have a similarity larger than 0.95. This shows it is hard to differentiate perturbed graphs from clean ones, since the generated perturbed graphs are stealthy under the constraint"

## [POSITIVE] Explanatory edge preservation constraint
Attack constraint that prevents deletion of the original explanatory edges ES from the perturbed graph, avoiding a trivial solution where the attacker simply removes ES.

**Delta**: ensures attack is non-trivial and realistic
**Condition**: Applied as a constraint in both loss-based and deduction-based attacks

**Evidence**: "The explanatory edges ES on the clean graph G=(V,E) should be kept in the perturbed graph G˜=(V,E˜). Otherwise, there is a trivial solution where the attacker can simply remove ES to fool the GNN explainer."

## [POSITIVE] GNN prediction faithfulness constraint
Ensures the GNN model still makes accurate predictions on the perturbed graph, so the attack only changes explanations without altering classification outcomes.

**Delta**: fraction of failed attacks (mis-predictions) is low, e.g., 4.2% on House for loss-based attack against GNNExplainer
**Condition**: Applied as a constraint across all attack methods and datasets

**Evidence**: "The GNN model f still makes accurate predictions on the perturbed graph. This ensures the model faithfulness... the fraction is low, indicating the perturbed graph does not change the prediction often."

## [POSITIVE] Perturbation budget constraint
Limits the number of perturbed edges to a budget ξ (default ξ=2), making the attack practical and resource-constrained.

**Delta**: with ξ=2, up to 70% explanatory edges changed; larger ξ yields better attack performance
**Condition**: Default setting ξ=2; studied across varying budgets in ablation

**Evidence**: "our attacks become more effective with larger ξ. This is because a larger ξ ensures the adversary has more capability to perturb the input graph... when perturbing only 2 edges, the explanatory edges can be 70% different from those without the attack."

## [NEGATIVE] Surrogate loss black-box attack
Uses a surrogate explanation loss (Cross Entropy loss + mask loss) when the true explanation loss is unknown, enabling a fully black-box attack scenario.

**Delta**: still better than random and kill-hot baselines but inferior to attacks with known explanation loss, e.g., 10.83% on House for GNNExplainer vs 20.02% with known loss
**Condition**: Black-box setting where attacker does not know the explanation loss

**Evidence**: "this attack still performs (much) better than the random and kill-hot baselines (see Table 2), but is inferior to our loss-based and deduction-based attacks with known explanation loss."

## [POSITIVE] Attack transferability to other explainer types
Perturbed graphs generated by attacking perturbation-based explainers are evaluated on decomposition-based (CAM), gradient-based (SA), surrogate-based (GraphSVX), and generation-based (GEM) explainers.

**Delta**: deduction-based attack achieves up to 55.25% overlap ratio on REDDIT for SA explainer; 'only 2 perturbed edges generated by our attacks ensure 50% explanatory edges in a state-of-the-art surrogate-based GNN explainer be changed'
**Condition**: Transfer from perturbation-based to other GNN explainer types; deduction-based attack shows stronger transferability than loss-based

**Evidence**: "all explainers are more vulnerable to the perturbed graphs generated by our (deduction-based) attack. Such results validate the promising transferability of our deduction-based attack is due to its produced attack edges are effective in general."

## [POSITIVE] Finite sample approximation of integral (N samples)
In the deduction-based attack, the integral over the mask learning trajectory is approximated using N discrete samples with constants βi based on a lower bound β.

**Delta**: performance difference within 5% across different N values; larger N shows slightly better performance
**Condition**: Deduction-based attack; studied in ablation on real-world datasets

**Evidence**: "Overall, the attack results are stable with different N's, e.g., the performance difference is within 5%. A larger N shows slightly better performance. One reason could be that larger N yields more accurate approximation of the integral."

## [NEUTRAL] Mask constant γ and β tuning
Both attacks fix mask values on explanatory edges to a constant (γ for loss-based, β-dependent for deduction-based) during optimization. The value of this constant affects attack performance.

**Delta**: best performance at intermediate values; too small (<0.6) or too large (1.0) values degrade performance
**Condition**: Ablation study on loss-based and deduction-based attacks

**Evidence**: "The results show our attacks obtain better performance when γ and β are not too large (e.g., 1.0) or not too smaller (e.g., <0.6). One reason is too small values underestimate the effect of explanatory edges, while too large values overestimate the effect."

## [NEGATIVE] Explanation size k impact
The number of explanatory edges k selected by the explainer affects attack difficulty; smaller k is easier to attack under a fixed perturbation budget.

**Delta**: attacks perform better on smaller k; larger k is harder to affect with limited ξ=2
**Condition**: Studied on real-world datasets MUTAG and REDDIT in ablation

**Evidence**: "We can see our attacks perform better on a smaller k. This is understandable, as it would be naturally more challenging to affect larger number of edges, with a limited perturbation budget ξ=2."

## [POSITIVE] Local computation graph subgraph processing
For large graphs, GNN explainers and attacks operate on the node's local computation graph (2-3 hop neighborhood subgraph) rather than the full graph, scaling down processing.

**Delta**: enables handling of large graphs like OGBN-Products (81.12% GCN accuracy)
**Condition**: Applied to large-scale graphs such as OGBN-Products

**Evidence**: "the current GNN explainers only use the node's local computation graph (i.e., a subgraph formed by the node's within 2 or 3-hop neighboring nodes and their connections) for explanation. This can significantly scale down the graph size to be processed. Our attacks also perform on this subgraph."

## [NEGATIVE] Structure difference detection defense
Defense using NetSim and DeltaCon similarity metrics to detect adversarially perturbed graphs by checking structural differences from clean graphs.

**Delta**: all perturbed graphs have similarity >0.95 with clean graphs, making detection ineffective
**Condition**: Empirical defense evaluated against deduction-based attack

**Evidence**: "We found all the perturbed graphs generated by our deduction-based attack and the respective clean graphs have a similarity larger than 0.95. This shows it is hard to differentiate perturbed graphs from clean ones"

## [NEGATIVE] API-only access defense
Defense that limits attacker knowledge by only providing API access to the GNN explainer, preventing direct access to model internals or explanation loss.

**Delta**: surrogate loss attack still achieves meaningful overlap ratios, e.g., 40.21% on House for GSAT
**Condition**: Empirical defense; evaluated using surrogate loss deduction-based attack

**Evidence**: "it is still possible for the attacker to break this defense. For instance, the attacker can use a surrogate explanation loss and perform our attack. Table 6 shows this surrogate loss based attack is somewhat effective."
